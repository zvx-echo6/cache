# 5. Notebooks

## What notebooks are

"Notebooks" is where you write things down on your phone. You keep several notebooks. Each one is made from a template:
- "Journal": dated entries about what happened. Sorted by date.
- "Log": something you do again and again, such as a generator test, with fields like run time. It has quick add, last done and due again.
- "Notes": plans, checklists and lists you keep updating.
- "Places": places with notes and photos, such as rally points, caches and water sources. They show on the map.

A fresh install has a Journal. You can make more notebooks from the same templates.

To open Notebooks, open "Notebooks" from "Modules" (or pin it to the bar). The screen lists your notebooks, each with how many entries it holds. "Log again" shows the logs you used last. "Search" looks across all your notebooks.

Cache can also read your notebooks when it answers, and you can ask it to add an entry. See the section Ask Cache to add to a notebook.

Private notebooks are left out of sync and sharing by default. See the section Private notebooks.

## Make a notebook

1. Open "Notebooks" and tap "New notebook".
2. Under "Start from", choose a template: "Journal", "Log", "Notes" or "Places". Each one says what it is for.
3. To start a log that is already set up, choose one under "Starter logs" instead.
4. Give the notebook a name. You cannot use the same name as another notebook: Cache says "You already have a notebook with this name."
5. Tap "Create".

To change a notebook later, open it and use "Edit notebook". There you can change its name and turn "Show on map" and "Private" on or off.

To remove a notebook, open it and tap "Delete notebook". A pop-up asks you to confirm and says how many entries go with it. This cannot be undone.

To put a notebook on "Home", open it and tap "Add to Home".

## Journal

A Journal notebook keeps dated entries: what happened, where, and what you did.

To add an entry:
1. Open the Journal and tap "New entry". (When it is empty the button says "Write an entry".)
2. Optional: type a "Title (optional)".
3. Write your text in the big box. It formats as you type. See the section Write with Markdown.
4. Tap "Add photo" to add a photo.
5. Tap "Done".

Cache saves as you type. If you leave before "Done", the journal shows a line such as "Unfinished entry · Tue 4:12 PM" with a "Continue" button.

Each entry has a time, which you can change, and a place. The place starts as where you are. If there is no position yet, the entry says "No position yet" and still saves.

With "Show on map" on, the journal's entries appear on the map.

To delete an entry, open it and choose "Delete". Cache says "Entry deleted" with "Undo".

If your phone is out of space, Cache says "Not saved. Your phone may be out of space." Free some space and tap "Try again". Your text is kept until then.

## Log notebooks

A Log notebook is for something you do again and again, such as testing a generator, rotating water, or noting a blood pressure reading. Each entry has the same fields.

To make a log:
1. Open "Notebooks" and tap "New notebook".
2. Choose "Log" under "Start from", or pick a ready-made one under "Starter logs".
3. For your own, tap "Add field" for each thing you want to record. For each field set its "Label" and its "Type". Types are text, number, yes or no, choice and place. A number can have a "Unit". A choice takes "Choices", one per line; the first is the default.
4. Optional: set "Due again" to every day, week, month and so on. The log then shows when it is due or overdue.
5. Tap "Create".

When, text, place and photos are always there. You do not add them.

To add an entry, tap "Add entry" in the log, or a row under "Log again" on the Notebooks screen. A quick form opens. Number, choice and yes or no start with your last entry's value. Change what is different and tap "Save". Cache says Saved to and the log's name, with "Undo".

The top of a log shows when it was "LAST DONE".

Once a log has entries, you cannot change a field's type. You can use "Hide field" to stop showing a field. Its old values are kept.

## Notes

A Notes notebook is for plans, checklists and lists you keep updating. Each note has a title and a body.

1. Open the notebook and tap "New entry".
2. Type a "Title (optional)".
3. Write in the body. Type `- [ ]` followed by a space and an item to make a checkbox. See the section Write with Markdown.
4. Tap "Done".

You can attach a photo to a note with "Add photo".

To find a note later, use "Search" on the Notebooks screen, or the search box on "Home", which also searches your notebooks.

## The Places notebook

Saved places live in the Places notebook. They used to be under Weather settings. They are not there any more.

To open it, open "Notebooks" and tap "Places".

The first row is always "Where I am", labelled "Follows the phone". It is fixed: you cannot delete it or move it. It has its own "Weather and alerts" switch with a gear, and its own marker toggle, like any other place.

The rows after it are your places, each with its own marker colour:
- Tap a place's marker (or the marker of "Where I am") to show or hide it on the map. A hidden place shows an outlined marker with a slash through it.
- Tap a place to open it.
- Press and hold a place to edit it.
- Tap "Add place" (a plus icon and the words) to add one.

Each row says a little about the place, for example whether weather is on and how many photos it has.

You can also save a place straight from the map: press and hold the map and choose "Save". See the section The map ring menu. From a place's card in "Maps", tap "Save place".

For the form that adds and edits a place, see the section Add or edit a place.

## Add or edit a place

Add place and Edit place are the same full screen.

To add a place, open the Places notebook and tap "Add place" (a plus icon and the words). To edit one, press and hold it in the list.

The screen has, from the top:
1. A map, with a search box. Search for an address, a place or coordinates. Tap the map to move the pin.
2. "Name".
3. "Marker colour": seven swatches. There is no red, because red means serious alerts. Each colour has a spoken name. If you do not choose, Cache picks one.
4. "Show on the map": whether the place's marker shows on the map.
5. "Weather and alerts": a switch. When it is on, a gear appears next to it.
6. "Notes": your text, in Markdown. See the section Write with Markdown.
7. "Photos": tap the plus to add one.
8. "Save". When you edit, there is also "Delete place", which asks you to confirm.

The gear opens "Alerts for" this place with three choices:
- "Same as your alert setting": follows "Settings" > "Notifications". This is the default.
- "Custom for this place": opens "How far out" and the kinds you want, such as fires, earthquakes and river levels. What a Custom place picks, within its distance, pops up even when "Pop up for" is "Serious only" or "None". It is silent in quiet hours unless it is serious.
- "Off": no alerts for this place.

A place with "Weather and alerts" on is watched. Cache keeps weather, alerts and fires for it.

## The map ring menu

Press and hold any spot on the map. A ring menu opens around your finger. The centre shows the place name and its coordinates. Tap outside the ring to close it.

The wedges are:
- "To here": directions to this spot.
- "From here": directions that start at this spot. The search opens so you can choose where to go. A chip says the route starts from the spot, and you can use it to go back to the phone's position. If a route is already shown, it is worked out again from this spot.
- "Add stop": adds this spot to the route on screen, after its other stops. It is shown only while a route is shown. A route can have up to 10 stops. Each stop is listed under the destination with a button to remove it.
- "Save": keep this spot in a notebook.
- "Measure": draws a dashed line from this spot. Each tap on the map adds a point. A bar shows the running total in your units. "Undo" removes the last point. "Done" ends the measurement.

Tap the centre of the ring to open the coordinate card for the spot. It shows the latitude and longitude, the grid reference, the Plus Code and the nearest town.

Tap "Save" and the ring changes into the notebook ring. It shows your notebooks, most used first: "Places", "Journal" and your logs. A ring holds at most six wedges: five notebooks and "More". "More" turns the ring to the next page, and the centre shows where you are, for example "2 of 3". The centre also has a back arrow. Tap it to go back.

Tap a notebook to open that notebook's full entry form at the spot you pressed. For "Places" it is the Add place screen with the pin already there.

If you have many notebooks, the ring gets more pages. Nothing else changes.

## Write with Markdown

Every notebook entry is written in Markdown. There are no formatting buttons. You type the marks, and the text formats as you type.

Some of the marks:
- Headings: start a line with `#`, then a space.
- Bold: put two asterisks round the words. Italic: one asterisk. Strikethrough: two tildes.
- Lists: start lines with `-` and a space, or `1.` and a space.
- Checkboxes: start a line with `- [ ]` and a space. Use `- [x]` for one that is ticked.
- Quotes: start a line with `>` and a space.
- Code: put backticks round the words.
- Links: put the words in square brackets, then the address in round brackets.
- Tables: separate columns with the `|` mark.
- A link to another entry: put its name in double square brackets.

The small "?" in the editor opens a one-screen cheat sheet with these marks.

Cache stores your text as plain Markdown.

## Private notebooks

A notebook or an entry can be marked "Private". Turn on "Private" in "Edit notebook", or in an entry.

What Private does:
- A private notebook is left out of sync and sharing between your own devices by default. It says "Left out of sharing and sync unless you choose to include it."
- To include a private notebook, open "Edit notebook". When the notebook is private, a switch "Include in sync and sharing" appears. It is off by default. Turn it on to include the notebook.

What Private does not do:
- It does not hide the notebook from the assistant. Once Cache is open, the assistant can read everything in your notebooks, private ones too.
- It is not a lock. Keep a screen lock on your phone.

Obsidian and Logseq folders are different. Private notebooks do go to a folder you connect. See the section Write notebooks to Obsidian or Logseq.

Some starter logs, such as medication and health ones, start as private. You can switch it off.

## Write notebooks to Obsidian or Logseq

Cache can keep your entries as Markdown files in a folder you choose, so Obsidian or Logseq can open them.

To connect a folder:
1. Open "Notebooks", open its menu and tap "Connections".
2. Under "Obsidian" or "Logseq", tap "Connect".
3. For Logseq, choose "Logseq with a folder of Markdown files" (Logseq 0.10 and earlier) or "Logseq 2".
4. Choose the folder. On Android, Cache uses Android's folder picker and keeps the permission you give it. On iPhone, Cache keeps the folder you pick as a bookmark, so it can open it again later.

What happens next:
- Obsidian: Cache keeps your entries as Markdown files in the folder. Open that folder as a vault in Obsidian. Changes in either place update the other.
- Logseq with a folder of Markdown files: Cache keeps your entries as blocks on the journal pages of a Logseq graph. Open that folder as a graph in Logseq. Changes in either place update the other.
- "Logseq 2": Cache writes your entries as Markdown for Logseq to import. Changes made in Logseq stay there. The row says "export only".
- Cache writes your text as you typed it, in Markdown. Each entry's details, such as its title, date and place, go in its properties. Photos are saved in the folder too.
- Cache can write to Markdown graphs only. If a Logseq graph is not Markdown, Cache says so.

Private notebooks: entries in private notebooks are written to Obsidian and Logseq folders like all others. They carry a private mark in their properties.

The row shows the folder's name and a status, such as Synced and the time, "Syncing...", "Paused." or "Can't reach the folder." The row's menu has "Sync now", "Pause" or "Resume", and "Disconnect".

If an entry changes in both places, Cache shows a card. Pick "Keep Cache's version" or "Keep the file's version". The other version is kept as a copy. If a file was removed in the folder, the card offers "Keep in Cache" or "Delete here too". If Cache can't read a file, it leaves it alone and you can tap "Ignore".

Your other devices are on the same "Connections" screen. See "Sharing and sync" in chapter 3.

## Ask Cache to add to a notebook

In "Ask" you can tell Cache to log something, for example: add a generator test, 20 minutes, under load. Cache can add to your Journal and to your log notebooks.

1. Ask in plain words.
2. Cache shows a card with what it will add. For a log it says Add to and the log's name. For the Journal it says "Add to Journal". The card lists the details, such as the values and the time.
3. Nothing changes until you tap the add button. "Not now" cancels.
4. After you tap, Cache says it was added, with a link to open it.

If you ask for something you have no log for, Cache says so and points you to "Notebooks". Cache does not make new logs for you. You make them yourself with "New notebook".

To fix a mistake before you confirm, tell Cache what to change. It replaces the card.
